# Oral Quiz — Student Guide

> **Public handout:** use the [illustrated Student guide](../public/site/student.html) and its
> [two-page PDF](../public/site/student-guide.pdf). This longer reference retains course-specific
> examples; the public edition distinguishes course choices from universal behavior.

A short, spoken quiz. You explain an idea out loud for a couple of minutes. It is graded
automatically. You can retake it as many times as you want — each retake gives you a **new
question**, so retaking is just extra practice. Your **most recent** upload is the one graded, and
you can re-activate an earlier one if it was better. This guide takes 2 minutes to read.

## What you'll need
- A phone or computer with a **microphone** (your laptop or phone is fine).
- A quiet-ish spot and a few minutes.
- A modern browser: **Safari** on iPhone, **Chrome** on Android, or Chrome/Firefox/Safari/Edge
  on a computer. Nothing to install.

## How it works
1. **Open the link.** In Gradescope, open the assignment — the quiz link is in its description.
   Tap it.
2. **Paste your quiz key.** That is the *only* thing you enter — no email, no password, no login.
   It is the sixteen-character code like `ABCD-EFGH-JKMN-PQRS` posted for you in **Brightspace**.
   Your browser remembers it, so you do this once.
   *Lost it?* Submit to the Gradescope assignment with no recording — the feedback shows your key.
   It belongs to you: don't pass it around, and don't take a quiz with someone else's. (It only
   opens questions; anything recorded with it is graded for **its owner**, not for whoever used it.)
3. **Tap "Allow mic & start."** Your browser asks to use the microphone — tap **Allow**.
   Recording begins *before* you see the question. That's on purpose.
4. **Say the code aloud.** Four digits appear (e.g. `4 7 1 9`). **Read them out loud,
   one at a time.** The question is deliberately held back for a few seconds so you can do this
   without rushing — the code stays on screen the whole time.
5. **Read the question and answer out loud.** The timer starts when the question appears, so
   reading the code costs you none of your answering time. A timer (usually **2 minutes per
   question**) counts down. Speak naturally, as if explaining to a classmate.
   **Nothing will interrupt you.** Just explain.
   When it reaches 0:00 you get a short grace period — the screen says *"finish your sentence"* —
   so you are never cut off mid-word. Don't rely on it; it is seconds, not minutes.
6. **Tap "I'm done with this question"** (or let the timer run out). The question disappears and
   **four new digits** appear. **Keep recording and read those aloud too.**
   - If your quiz has **more than one question**, the next one appears right after those digits —
     with its own fresh timer. The screen says *Question 2 of 3* so you always know where you are.
     Repeat steps 5–6 until you have answered them all.
   - After the **last** question, reading the digits is the final step: tap **Finish & submit**.
   The codes are how the system confirms you answered live, all the way through. **Every set has
   to be spoken** — that is the one thing students most often forget.
7. **Download the `.zip`** and **upload it to Gradescope**. That's your submission. Done.

> **More than one question?** Your instructor decides how many. Each question gets its own timer
> and its own "I'm done" — and each is graded on its own, so a great answer to question 1 does not
> cover a skipped question 2. It is all **one continuous recording**: don't stop it, don't reload
> the page, and don't go back — once you tap "I'm done with this question", that answer is final.

## Retakes — use them
- Fumbled the recording, or want a better score? Tap **"New question / retake"** and go again.
  You get a **different question** each time.
- **Your most recent upload is the one that counts.** Gradescope grades whichever submission is
  *active*, and that is the newest one by default.
- So if a retake goes **worse** than an earlier attempt, you are not stuck with it: open
  **Submission History** on the assignment and **activate** the attempt you want graded. Two clicks,
  and which one counts is entirely up to you.
- Retaking still costs you nothing — just remember to re-activate your best one if the newest is not
  it.
- **One thing to know:** if you open a question and walk away *without finishing*, you'll wait
  about **5 minutes** before you can open another. Finishing an attempt lets you retake straight
  away. This just stops people from flipping through questions hunting for an easy one — answer
  the one you get, and you'll never notice the rule.

## What we're looking for
- **Understanding, explained in your own words** — the "why," not memorized formulas or code.
- It's fine to think out loud, correct yourself, and be informal. Grading rewards the *ideas*.
- You do **not** need to be polished. A clear, correct explanation beats a rehearsed one.

## Why there's no login
Your quiz key *is* your identity for the quiz page — which is why you never type an email there.
The quiz server only ever sees the key, and it cannot work out who you are from it. Your work is
matched to you at the moment you **upload to Gradescope**, where you are already signed in.

So: nothing about your identity leaves your browser, and your recording still lands on your own
Gradescope submission. (Some courses run without keys; if the page asks for your email instead,
it is scrambled in your browser before anything is sent.)

## Please answer the question, and nothing else

**Don't say your name, your student ID, your address, or anything else personal.** Nothing outside
the question is needed — not to identify you, not for grading. Gradescope already knows who you
are.

The reason is simple: everything you say is turned into text, and that text is what gets graded.
Personal details you mention become part of it. If some slip in anyway, they are stripped out
before your answer is graded and it makes **no difference to your score** — but the surest way to
keep them private is not to say them.

## Privacy — where your audio goes

Precisely, so you can decide what you are comfortable with:

- **Your recording is made in your browser and uploaded only to Gradescope**, with the rest of your
  submission. It is not sent anywhere else and it is not stored on any other server.
- **The quiz website never receives your audio.** It gets a *fingerprint* — a hash, a short string
  of characters the audio cannot be reconstructed from — plus a few timestamps. That is enough to
  certify that you answered live, and it is not enough to hear anything.
- **It also never receives your name or email.** Your quiz key is your identity there, and the
  server cannot work out who you are from it.
- **After you upload**, software running inside Gradescope turns your audio into text. The **text**
  of your answer is then sent to Purdue's GenAI Studio to be graded against the rubric. **Your
  audio is never sent there.**
- **Your instructor** can listen to the audio and read the transcript, in Gradescope, alongside
  your other work.

## Tips & troubleshooting
- **Don't let your phone lock.** This is the one thing most likely to cost you an attempt. You will
  be talking for a couple of minutes without touching the screen, and most phones lock after 30
  seconds — which pauses the recording while the quiz's clock keeps running. The page tries to keep
  the screen awake for you, but not every phone lets it. Before you start, set **auto-lock to
  a few minutes or Never** (iPhone: Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock; Android:
  Settings → Display → Screen timeout), or just tap the screen every so often while you speak.
- **Stay on the page until you have downloaded the .zip.** Don't switch apps, take a call, or swipe
  back mid-attempt. Your recording lives only in that browser tab until you download it — if you
  leave before then, there is nothing for us to recover.
- **Use earbuds/headphones** if you can — cleaner audio, better grading.
- **"Microphone blocked"?** Allow mic access for the site and reopen the link. On iPhone:
  tap **"AA"** in Safari's address bar → Website Settings → Microphone → Allow. On Android
  Chrome: tap the lock icon → Permissions → Microphone.
- **"Link expired"?** The class link is refreshed each term. Grab the current one from the
  assignment description in Gradescope.
- **"This recording was not certified under your identity"?** The email you typed doesn't match
  the one Gradescope knows you by. The feedback tells you exactly which address to use — retake
  and type that one.
- **“UNCERTIFIED BACKUP”?** The page saved your audio but could not get the quiz server's signed
  certificate. Download and upload the backup so staff can see what happened, but it receives an
  automatic 0. Make a fresh certified attempt if you can — that is strongly preferred. If you
  cannot retry, contact the instructional staff; they can listen carefully and may manually
  override the 0, though full credit will normally require a certified attempt.
- **Lost connection while recording?** If the page can preserve a completed recording it offers
  the uncertified backup above; otherwise retake for a fresh question.
- **Say every code.** The digits before the first question, the digits between questions, and the
  digits at the very end all have to be spoken out loud. If one is missing the system can't confirm
  you answered live and the attempt scores 0. A few seconds each, and the feedback tells you which
  one was missing if it happens. Read them **one digit at a time** — "eight, zero, four" — not as a
  whole number, and pause for a beat first rather than talking over them.
- **"You opened a question and did not finish it"?** That's the 5-minute retake wait. Finish an
  attempt instead of abandoning it and you can retake immediately.

## Your recording is kept
Your audio stays in Gradescope with the rest of your work, and your instructor may listen to it —
for grading questions, or to check that the voice is consistent across your attempts. If anything
looks unclear, you may be asked to explain the same idea in office hours or over Zoom. Nothing is
decided automatically.

## Expectations (the short version)
- Speak for about 2 minutes per question. Read every set of digits aloud, one digit at a time.
  Download, upload.
- Answer the question and nothing else — no names, no ID numbers, nothing personal.
- Unlimited retakes, fresh question each time. Your **most recent** upload is the one graded — if an
  earlier attempt was better, re-activate it in Submission History.
- Explain the idea, not the algebra. Your voice, your understanding.

## If you have extended-time accommodations

On the start screen, tap **"I have DRC accommodations for extended time"** and choose the extension
you have been granted — 1.25x, 1.5x, 1.75x or 2x. Your countdown on every question is multiplied by
it.

Three things worth knowing:

- **Pick what you have actually been granted.** Your selection is checked against your
  accommodations in Gradescope when your work is graded.
- **You are never penalised for taking less.** Choosing a smaller extension than you hold, choosing
  none, or simply finishing early is always fine and is never held against you.
- **If we cannot confirm your accommodation automatically, nothing bad happens.** Your work is
  graded normally, your instructor is told so they can check, and your score is not affected. You
  may see a note saying exactly that. There is nothing you need to do.
- **Your accommodation is not stored on the quiz server.** It travels inside your own submission to
  Gradescope, which already knows about it.
- **Set the accommodation up with your instructor or your disability resource office** — it is
  arranged there, not on this page. If it is not in place yet, leave the option alone and talk to
  them.

If you are entitled to more than 2x, tell your instructor before the quiz: the page cannot offer
more than 2x, so they will need to arrange it another way.

## If you submit late

Your instructor may set a late policy for the course — for example, one day late costs 20% of the
total, and nothing is accepted after that. If a deduction is applied you will see a note saying
exactly how much and why.

**If you had an approved extension, contact your instructor.** The deduction is applied
automatically from the assignment's due date, and it cannot see extensions granted to you
individually. Your instructor can override the score.

## If something goes wrong

The page will show a short **error code** like `E_NET_CLOSING`. Read it out or screenshot it when
you tell your instructor — it says exactly which step failed, and turns a long conversation into a
quick fix.

Codes starting `E_NET_` mean the quiz server could not be reached. The page already retried several
times before showing you that, so it is usually a network problem at your end: check wifi, then
retake. Nothing is submitted until you upload to Gradescope, so a failed attempt costs you nothing
but the time.
